Complete Guide to United States Time Zones & Daylight Saving

Understanding the structure of United States time zones is essential for scheduling meetings, managing remote teams, and coordinating international travel. The continental United States spans four primary standard time zones, with additional zones covering Alaska, Hawaii, and US territories.

1. Eastern Standard Time (EST / EDT)

Eastern Standard Time covers the East Coast of the US, including major financial centers like New York, Miami, Atlanta, and Washington, D.C. It operates at UTC-5 during standard time and shifts to UTC-4 during Daylight Saving Time (EDT).

2. Central Standard Time (CST / CDT)

Central Standard Time is 1 hour behind Eastern Time (UTC-6 standard, UTC-5 DST). Key cities in this zone include Chicago, Houston, Dallas, and Minneapolis.

3. Mountain Standard Time (MST / MDT)

Mountain Standard Time is 2 hours behind Eastern Time (UTC-7 standard, UTC-6 DST). It covers states such as Colorado, Utah, and Montana. Note that Arizona operates on MST year-round without observing Daylight Saving Time (except for the Navajo Nation).

4. Pacific Standard Time (PST / PDT)

Pacific Standard Time covers the US West Coast, including Los Angeles, San Francisco, Seattle, and Las Vegas. It runs at UTC-8 during standard time and UTC-7 during Daylight Saving Time.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What are the primary time zones in the United States?

The continental United States uses four main time zones: Eastern Time (ET), Central Time (CT), Mountain Time (MT), and Pacific Time (PT).

Is Florida in the Eastern Time Zone?

Most of Florida is in Eastern Standard Time (EST/EDT), while parts of the western panhandle fall under Central Standard Time (CST/CDT).

How many time zones are there in the United States?

The US covers 6 standard time zones across states (Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific, Alaska, and Hawaii-Aleutian).
